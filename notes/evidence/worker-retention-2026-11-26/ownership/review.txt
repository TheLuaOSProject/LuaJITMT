The active-worker memory growth has three separate sources. Empty plain
arenas remain attached to dead allocators. Small native ownership records
can legitimately retain a second arena. The whole dead TG body also stays
allocated until a globally quiescent transfer/reclamation pass. Fixing only
empty-arena trimming must not be reported as a full worker-churn bound.

1. Retention of empty plain arenas is a concrete production gap.

lj_tg.c1001-1013 admits runtime dead-TG reclamation only with n_threads==1,
mt_live==0, mt_entering==0, n_workers==0, worker_active==0, no handshake,
and the current actor being main_tg. Thus one parked managed peer, or a
configured GC worker pool, blocks allocator transfer indefinitely. This
is an intentional safety restriction of the existing raw-owner protocol,
not evidence that merely removing those predicates would be safe.

Dead allocators are prepared by safepoint_apply_dead_allocators at
lj_safepoint.c1278-1295. RESET_ALLOC force-drains their intrusive queues
and plain late bits (393-425), prepares only TRAVERSABLE, and restores
PLAIN. Production sweep at lj_gc2.c6331-6488 processes traversable and
Huge storage. Its dead empty trim at6440-6443 applies only to a just-finished
traversable empty_reclaimed head, not owned[PLAIN]. A dead allocator's
empty plain capacity is consequently neither allocatable by a live owner
nor unmapped until global transfer/terminal teardown.

Parent-owned baseline census independently corroborates this mechanism:
/tmp/lj-worker-plain-retention-20261126-4u6_n8fl/baseline.stdout
16 waves x4 workers, each followed by3 full collections while a parked
sentinel remained alive: dead_tgs64, dead_plain_arenas128, empty64,
blocks85, late0, actors0, queues0 at the last collected snapshot.
After releasing the sentinel, disabling the GC pool and5 full collections:
dead_tgs0, main/live plain arenas130, empty65. Logical heap fell from
2137.34KiB to167.96KiB. This transfers capacity; it does not trim it.
These are parent measurements, not a new measurement by this source audit.

2. One likely live raw block per worker is finalizer-registration metadata.

buffer.new invokes lj_udata_finreg_mt_rooted at lib_buffer.c323. Registration
allocates GC2FinRegUDataNode through lj_mem_new_nothrow on the current
worker at lj_gc2.c13994-14001. The default x64 node is32 bytes:
GCRef obj, next, retired_next, uint32 active plus alignment (lj_obj.h827).
The retired list explicitly retains these nodes until teardown
(lj_obj.h2266; lj_gc2.c13967-13971,9206-9208). Physical frees appear only
in lj_gc2_fini at2121-2141. A single retired record can pin an otherwise
empty64KiB worker arena after buffer:free and all userdata finalizers finish.
Confirm the census survivor by matching exact block-start addresses against
the active and retired FINREG lists; source proves the retention policy,
but does not identify every one of the85 measured starts.

Runtime FINREG freeing is not a one-line addition to an existing writer.
Registration walks raw nodes with point-in-time mem_registered probes
(13987-13992). forget does likewise (14093-14112). Retire publishes the
separate retired-list membership before best-effort active-spine unlink
(14042-14083); a retired node can still be reachable from the active head.
The current terminal lifetime protects both borrowed next pointers and
failed unlinks. Earlier reclamation must cover every walker/publisher with
a lifetime protocol and complete unlinking before retiring the storage.
The current SMR writer by itself does not supply that missing discipline.

3. Dead TG bodies account for substantial independently retained metadata.

threading.spawn allocates sizeof(TGState) from the spawning parent before
child attachment (lib_threading.c2103). Therefore its body usually appears
in the main parent's plain arenas, not the child's owned plain list.
TGState embeds two8KiB SSB buffers plus dispatch/hotcount, allocator,
JIT and FFI metadata (lj_tg.h292-425). The parent's approximately30KiB/TG
logical-accounting growth is consistent with those bodies; exact sizeof
can be added to a diagnostic census without interpreting raw payloads.

After a joined handle dies, its finalizer marks a still-registered Lua TG
TGF_DEFER_FREE and clears the userdata's TG/child pointers
(lib_threading.c1512-1547). Actual TG finalization/free occurs only after
the globally gated registry/allocator reclaim (lj_tg.c1122-1218). Stable
TG slots also remain allocated until global shutdown, by explicit policy
(lj_tg.c1212-1218,1273-1310). Early TG removal is broader than arena trim:
lj_tg_find_owner at1313-1325 returns a raw TG pointer, and lj_mem_free
routes through it (lj_gc.c4767-4787,5973-5978). Other live mutators are
currently excluded from racing a transfer by the global admissibility
predicates. Retained SSB nodes and stable registry borrows are additional
negative vetoes, not substitutes for that exclusion.

4. The detach/result ownership order appears consistent.

Worker prepare attaches its fresh TG before claiming/using the child state
(lib_threading.c965-989). Stack rehome allocates into its own allocator
(lj_state.c149-180). Completion publishes results through the child stack
and result-GC handoff, then cleanup releases state ownership, detaches,
clears TLS, drops mt_live, and finally publishes DONE (lib_threading.c
1067-1117). The last drop therefore cannot make transfer legal while the
worker still needs its state/TG for cleanup.

lj_tg_detach checks physical actor ownership and all active native/JIT/raw
scopes, closes stable registry admission, flushes roots/SSB/accounting,
frees tmpbuf, clears Lua-state and TLS publications, retires the actor,
then publishes DEAD and decrements n_threads once (813-924). A second
detach returns immediately819-820. Public threading_detach is also a
no-op after TLS has been cleared (lib_threading.c2430-2446).

join copies results under a child-state ownership claim and deliberately
keeps the child stack for subsequent joins (lib_threading.c1403-1423).
THREAD userdata traversal marks that child (lj_gc2.c20996-21017). Therefore
exit must not free the child stack or a worker-created shared buffer simply
because its allocating TG is dead. Child stack release belongs to the
ordinary THREAD destructor/preparation path (lj_state.c877-938).

Only root-anchor overflow blocks are an obvious remaining TG-private plain
cache: they allocate at lj_tg.c128/154 and free only at245-257 during fini.
Releasing them at detach would need root_anchor_top==0 and a proven owner/
scanner boundary. Ordinary buffer helpers use the embedded slots, so this
does not explain one persistent block per worker in the simple workload.
tmpbuf already releases at detach868. JIT event snapshot/root caches use
libc malloc/free (lj_trace.c1597-1606), and dynamic SSB nodes use libc
malloc/free; their embedded16KiB buffers cannot be independently freed.

5. No lost durable plain-free wake was found under current transfer rules.

Late intent publishes its bit before remote_pending=1 (lj_arena.c5857-5865).
Hinted drains exchange pending to0 before scanning (6475-6492), preserving
a later producer's wake. Blocked queue/gate and side-ownership cases rearm
pending (6243-6255,6388-6417). Transfer force-drains source and carries a
residual wake to destination (8377-8383,8443-8444). Current global transfer
exclusion prevents a lawful producer from appearing behind that exchange;
weakening it would require redesigning wake routing as well as TG lifetime.

Dead owners have no ordinary allocation consumer. RESET_ALLOC executes
once per prepare_epoch/cycle (lj_safepoint.c405-425), while sweep wake only
wakes a worker during SWEEP (lj_gc2.c2183-2189). A plain free or last-reader
release after the dead owner's checkpoint can therefore wait until another
full cycle or transfer; the bit remains durable. A bounded dead-plain sweep
step should drain the exact candidate before trying empty certification,
so it need not add another whole cycle of physical retention.

6. Suggested bounded empty-plain trimming design.

Use gc2_sweep_reclaim_enter/leave (lj_gc2.c6130-6192), the same physical
writer used by the existing dead traversable trim. Do not unmap inside
safepoint_apply_dead_allocators, which holds ordinary SMR read1285-1295.
Only act on DEAD+ARENA_INTERNAL sources; never mutate a live owner's bins.

Charge one candidate arena examination per owner-progress budget unit.
Use a dead-allocator cursor/pass epoch so a nonempty or reader-blocked head
cannot hide empty successors and repeated steps do not rescan from head.
Explicitly account pending scans in sweep_pending if a cycle promises to
finish the pass. A paused reader must make its candidate defer, not wait
or prevent independent candidates from progressing.

For each candidate in owned[PLAIN], exclude lifecycle transitions; optionally
drain its intrusive and late queues first using existing owner helpers.
An early bitmap check can avoid closing admission for obviously live arenas.
Then arena_unmap_claim2053 closes fresh admission and requires zero existing
counts/no writer seal. Under that claim recheck every block and late word0,
arena_unmap_side_empty2115 (recovery/root/lifetime/dtor/token/descriptor),
gcprep_pending0, and remote_freeNULL. An exact retained plain destructor
already vetoes terminal claim through its SEALED gate; a defensive explicit
plain_destructNULL check is also reasonable. mark[] need not be zero: free
run boundaries live there. Do not mistake remote_pending1 alone for live
storage; it is only an advisory allocator-wide hint.

Only exact registry deletion (arena_registry_delete7080) grants the final
unmap permission, including readers entering via SWEEP_STABLE and handing
off from the registry to an arena count. On refusal, restore terminal
admission and preserve the linked candidate for later work. On success,
unlink the owned node, decrement owned_count, clear a matching bump pointer,
and clear all dead plain bins/binmask before unmapping. Clearing bins is
safe for a dead source; transfer later rebuilds all free runs from bitmaps
(7598-7612,8424). Do not leave any bin or cursor pointing into an unmapped
arena. Preserve a remaining arena's unused bump-tail boundary for transfer.

7. Meaningful deterministic acceptance cases.

Keep a real managed sentinel parked on a public channel while repeated
short workers create/churn buffers, join, validate returned bytes, free/drop
results and handles, and run full GC. Record empty arenas, live block
starts, late/queue/count state, dead TG count, logical bytes, and RSS/VM.
Compare retained handles/repeated join against dropped handles explicitly.
Classify raw survivors by FINREG metadata, child stack, root-anchor overflow
and unknown; do not infer a leak from block count alone. Empty trimming must
reduce dead empty arenas while retaining meaningful live results and readers.

For a deterministic physical helper fixture, create a DEAD allocator whose
plain list has a retained reader/registered locator on candidateA and an
independently empty candidateB behind it. Under the real sweep writer,
verify A stays mapped and B unmaps while the reader remains paused; verify
ordinary SMR readers veto the writer, exact registry readers veto only A,
and every registry/bin/bump/count remains consistent. Then release the
reader, perform a later bounded step and prove A becomes reclaimable.
Include a nonempty FINREG-pinned arena and an explicit late-free target to
prove the new helper cannot free either before its exact owner drains.

Finally release the sentinel/pool, reclaim TGs and allocate from transferred
capacity; verify correct owner_tid routing, no stale bins/bump links, no
double n_threads decrement on repeated detach, retained join results, and
lua_close completion. Empty trimming alone should be accepted/reported only
as that narrower improvement until FINREG and TG metadata lifetimes are
addressed with their own reader and transfer proof.
