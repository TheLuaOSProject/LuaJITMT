Plain allocator reader-progress performance controls, Linux x86_64

The candidate preserves bounded buffer storage reuse and shows no live-heap growth scaling regression in these controls. Automatic buffer refills are effectively unchanged. The policy with a full GC every 20,000 cycles has a roughly 4% higher median process mean, with individual paired ratios spanning a small improvement to a 4.9% slowdown. This narrow measurement does not establish general performance parity or stock-LuaJIT parity.

Live heap growth

The unchanged direct allocator control retains 960-byte allocations across 16, 128, 1024 and 4096 nominal arenas, then verifies every byte sentinel. Five paired fresh processes per size all passed. Median paired candidate/baseline ratios are 1.0363, 0.9842, 0.9962 and 0.9894. The 4096 first baseline process is a large wall-time outlier, yielding pair 0.3416; it is retained and must not be interpreted as a candidate speedup. The other 4096 ratios are 0.9894, 0.9811, 1.0034 and 1.0299. Full samples and per-size summaries are in live-growth.json and live-growth-summary.json. These timings include mapping/page costs and scheduler delay, unlike buffer work's process CPU timings.

Reproducibility and scope

All 60 runtime invocations exited zero with empty stderr. Build commands, compiler output, dependency files, exact workload hashes, complete source/build manifests and raw command/output rows are retained. Runtime inputs, executable/library hashes and workload hashes were reverified after measurements. Baseline and candidate runtime input manifests differ only in the two arena source/header paths. CPU 8 was released after the last timing.

This is a single-mutator memory/performance control, not a full nonblocking proof, multiworker memory bound, sustained application workload, or Windows/macOS release validation. The project still requires the independent correctness/progress fixtures and broader completion plan.
