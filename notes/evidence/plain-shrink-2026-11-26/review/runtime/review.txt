Problem and resulting behavior
------------------------------
The previous small raw shrink could publish FREEING on its original start,
blocking new readers of the same allocation while paused. A failed writer
claim fell back to allocation/copy/free, which could fail even for a shrink.
An earlier side-owner check could also return NULL before the shrink branch.
lj_mem_realloc turns any positive-size NULL into a memory error. Lua's allocator
contract assumes nonfailure when old size is at least new size:
https://www.lua.org/manual/5.1/manual.html#lua_Alloc

The new positive small PLAIN shrink keeps p and the entire original physical
allocation. When the rounded logical cell count decreases, it sets a start-only
oversize bit in the otherwise unused PLAIN cdata bitmap. It does not acquire a
writer gate, publish FREEING, alter block/mark boundaries, or touch payload.
The branch precedes side-owner rejection for a valid caller-owned allocation.
Invalid alignment, missing allocation, huge shape, late intent and FREEING
still reject; concurrent free/realloc of the same allocation is not the
single-owner realloc contract. Existing body readers are compatible.

Publication and metadata review
-------------------------------
1. src/lj_arena.c:9146 validates the small PLAIN start before the accessor.
   src/lj_arena.h:1371 and :1377 provide named accessors. A release fence plus
   atomic OR publishes the bit; later acquire observations and ordinary caller
   ownership handoff preserve it. Repeated shrinks keep the bit. A shrink within
   the same rounded cell count needs no new flag but does not clear an old one.
   The exact-arena test hook runs immediately after publication.

2. The cdata plane retains its original meaning on TRAVERSABLE arenas. Typed
   GC2 consumers require traversable, READY and/or lifetime authority before
   treating coverage as a GC identity. Raw storage leases ignore this plane.
   The flag creates no type, root, recovery, destructor or lifetime authority.
   GCAhdr and GCArena layout, offsets and first usable cell are unchanged.

3. Full free-run publication clears cdata/oversize coverage for the complete
   reusable range. Allocation still rejects stale coverage and does not add
   new side-plane RMWs. Rebuild treats live block boundaries as occupied; it
   does not turn an oversized live allocation's suffix into reusable space.
   The production collector does not semantically mark-sweep PLAIN raw blocks.

Physical extent and all free routes
----------------------------------
4. src/lj_arena.c:9025 handles owner-local and retained-destructor completion.
   Only after obtaining the existing exact writer authority does it recover a
   flagged allocation's full physical cell count. The ordinary full-span
   ownership preflight consequently includes the retained suffix. A bad extent
   is retained as durable late intent, never truncated to logical osize.
   The existing exact destructor token and duplicate-free rules are unchanged.

5. src/lj_arena.c:6167 routes flagged remote frees to the admitted late path
   before writing an intrusive node. The producer does not read the owner's
   private bump cursor and does not enqueue logical length as physical length.
   The durable late bit precedes the existing owner wake and admission release.
   A drain defeated by the producer/reader count rearms; a late publication
   behind a scan leaves a later wake. The last foreign release does not mutate
   owner-local bins. Existing frozen-cohort drain rules are unchanged.

6. Deferred free, quarantine-side-owner routing and failed writer claims
   already preserve a late start without an intrusive size record. Their owner
   drain therefore recovers the whole original extent. The six focused routes
   are owner free, remote free, deferred free, destructor completion, allocf
   free, and direct realloc-to-zero. A subsequent growth copies logical bytes
   and sends the old allocation through the same physical free paths. Small
   retained allocations remain small under the current size discriminator.

7. src/lj_arena.c:6358 recovers extent from the next block|mark boundary, capped
   by the owner's active PLAIN bump cursor only when that cursor is after the
   start. This preserves allocations later than a reused interior bump window.
   The original physical allocation is at most 1,024 16-byte cells. A valid
   extent requires at most approximately 17 boundary-word observations.

8. Dropping a source bump cursor requires a persistent tail boundary.
   arena_publish_bump_run already preserves one on refusal. Transfer previously
   ignored arena_set_free_run refusal and then discarded its cursor. The patch
   at src/lj_arena.c:8438 preserves bitmap boundaries before that discard.
   The fixture places a root LINKING veto at tail+1 and proves that the exact
   old tail, full allocation extent and eventual reuse survive transfer.

Tests reviewed
--------------
tests/t-arena-plain-shrink.c uses real registered storage leases and payload
checks. It verifies a paused shrink permits new full-span observational and
marking leases; old bytes remain unchanged while held. It first proves an
actual allocation refusal, then shrinks 4096 -> 1025 -> 17 -> 1 without another
allocation attempt while readers, another target's retained destructor and
root/recovery metadata remain held. It checks all six free routes, exact full
run lengths, address reuse, repeated shrink/grow, an active tail without a
sentinel, a reused earlier bump window, and refused transfer-tail publication.

Existing tests were updated to require retained full bytes and full physical
free instead of a moved allocation or an immediately reusable suffix. Their
reader-lifetime assertions were retained. The public reader-progress fixture
now pauses at the shrink hook and requires the same target's old full-span
lease to succeed before resume. No test/contract mismatch was found. A separate
same-cell/equal-size positive shrink case was suggested as optional coverage;
it was not a condition of this source verdict.

Discovered and corrected standalone-header regression
-----------------------------------------------------
The initial accessor definitions used lj_assertX inside lj_arena.h. That header
includes lj_def.h but not lj_obj.h, which declares lj_assert_fail. Translation
units that included only the lightweight arena header failed assertion builds.
The original syntax checks compiled arena.c, which includes lj_obj.h first,
so they did not expose this mistake. Root's standalone fixture matrix did.

The correction removes those two header assertions and documents caller
validation. All production accesses already perform the required checks. No
heavy include was introduced, and runtime behavior is unchanged.

Memory and unresolved scope
---------------------------
A live shrunken small allocation can retain its complete original capacity,
up to 16 KiB, while logical GC accounting decreases. The bound is per original
allocation, not a process-wide constant. Many long-lived shrunk allocations can
therefore use substantially more memory than their logical sizes. This review
contains no new performance/RSS measurement and makes no stock-memory-parity
claim. Huge shrink remains an active nonfailure obligation; ordinary small
production shrink definitions currently have no callers, but that reachability
fact was not used to dismiss the generic small allocator contract.

Next design: huge and mixed-class raw shrink (NOT IMPLEMENTED)
-------------------------------------------------------------
A. Retain the physical raw Huge allocation and its HugeTab capacity on positive
   shrink. Logical osize/nsize remain with the caller and GC accounting. Return
   p after metadata-only validation, without BUSY, a body lease, allocation or
   payload/header access. Counted readers, including saturation, should not
   cause a valid shrink to fail. Missing, typed, terminal, competing-resize and
   destructive-admission-closed identities must still reject.

B. Classify existing allocator bases by immutable address geometry:
   ((uintptr_t)p & LJ_ARENA_MASK) == sizeof(GCAhdr).
   Huge payload bases have that offset; small allocations start at
   LJ_AFIRST_CELL. GC2 storage leases already use this distinction. Update
   owner free, direct realloc, allocf free and allocf realloc. Add explicit
   huge-shape rejection to small-only remote/deferred/destructor/quarantine
   APIs. Remote free currently admits before cell validation, so this guard is
   particularly important for a physically huge pointer with small osize.

C. Locate the exact HugeTab slot with bounded hugetab_search, then use one
   la_load128_acq to validate coherent address/capacity/flags. This existing
   primitive returns a snapshot with no CAS retry loop. A reader-count change
   must not become false shrink OOM through a failed no-op validation CAS.
   The snapshot is the no-op shrink LP. Retain current concurrent same-object
   growth/free arbitration; valid realloc ownership remains required.

D. Store immutable physical mapsize in a size_t union occupying the existing
   sweep_epoch/live_cells pair. Current production uses of these fields are
   small-arena operations except huge_map's unused truncated live_cells store.
   This preserves the 128-byte header and full size_t direct-allocation range.
   Direct huge unmap and direct same-mapping tests must use physical mapsize
   because logical osize can fall below the huge threshold. Prefer retaining
   authoritative HugeTab-capacity-driven lj_arena_huge_unmap_claimed: existing
   callers provide consumed slot capacity or unchanged sizeof(GG_State), and
   terminal cleanup deliberately avoids new header dependence.

E. Pass logical osize into arena_allocf_realloc_huge and copy min(osize,nsize),
   never min(stored capacity,nsize). Growth can retain its current BUSY and
   reader-sensitive move policy. A successful same-mapping growth must publish
   max(old capacity,nsize), so a grow after shrink cannot reduce capacity.

F. Keep typed HugeTab sizes unchanged. Raw leases use capacity as their physical
   bound; cdata exact-size validation only admits traversable entries. The GC
   huge live-byte estimate filters TRAVERSABLE. Logical allocation accounting
   is already performed from caller sizes, and deferred raw huge unmap does
   not subtract capacity again.

G. Preserve current growth BUSY/preemption tests. Add retained-reader and
   injected-failure huge shrink tests across both map-size and huge/small
   thresholds, a paused metadata-snapshot shrink, logical-prefix-only growth
   copying, and exact original unmap length through direct, deferred, transfer
   and terminal routes. Stale-pointer tests must run after the retained object
   is actually freed, including calls with its reduced logical size.

This huge design can retain an arbitrarily large original mapping for a small
logical allocation. It requires explicit memory measurements and a separate
reviewed implementation. It is not part of the source identities above.
