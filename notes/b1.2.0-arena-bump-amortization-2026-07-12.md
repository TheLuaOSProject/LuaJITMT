# b1.2.0 arena bump amortization (2026-07-12)

## Scope

This checkpoint removes a superlinear single-owner allocation cost without
changing allocation identity, arena ownership, lifetime/root lanes, or remote
free correctness. It is the allocator-only portion of the table-allocation
performance investigation composed after `81a84fba`.

The optional exact HugeTab recovery-lane counter from the same experiment is
not included. It improved the active-GC new-key microbenchmark by only about
2.4 percent and added global state/protocol surface unrelated to the dominant
cost. The smaller allocator change has a direct and independently measurable
benefit.

## Change

An available private bump window now remains the allocation source when no
already-published size-class bin can satisfy the request. The allocator no
longer adopts reclaimed arenas or scans every remote-free queue on each small
allocation merely to rediscover that those queues are empty. Published bins
still precede bump allocation, preserving the established reuse order.

When the old bump window is exhausted, the allocator keeps the previous
protocol: publish its remainder, consult bins, adopt one reclaimed arena, drain
remote frees, and map a fresh arena only if those sources are empty. A rebuilt
free-run tail can become the next private bump window after the complete range
passes the existing lifetime/root/recovery validation and its old boundaries
are cleared.

`arena_remote_free_drain_one()` also performs an acquire empty-head check before
claiming a plain arena generation. A racing remote publisher either defeats the
later ownership claim through `remote_active` or leaves durable queue work for
a later opportunistic drain. No remote free makes storage reusable until the
ordinary drain commits its block/bin transition.

## Performance

Pinned Linux/x86-64, JIT-enabled, GC-stopped insertion of unique dynamic string
keys measured as follows after composition:

| Insertions | Time |
| ---: | ---: |
| 200,000 | 391 ns/op |
| 400,000 | 404 ns/op |
| 800,000 | 440 ns/op |

The clean pre-change checkpoint measured approximately 1,540, 2,241, and 3,940
ns/op at the same sizes. The change therefore removes the runaway queue/bitmap
scan behavior and leaves near-linear cost. With GC active, the isolated delta
is intentionally modest because runtime string retention and GC2 root work are
the remaining dominant costs; those are separate b1.2.0 blockers.

## Verification and known independent failures

The isolated candidate passed the arena sweep fixture, GC2 recovery in default
and assertion/paranoia builds, the full GC2 paranoia stock-test set, focused
string-table/table lifetime tests, and target-only Clang ASan allocation,
sweep, and recovery smokes. The composed shared checkpoint repeats the arena,
recovery, string-table, table publication/retire, and allocation benchmarks.

`m5_tab_forward_filter`, intermittent `m5_tab_resize_stress` corruption, and
the full threading API `lua_gc` hang reproduce on the untouched pre-change
checkpoint. They remain release blockers and are not attributed to or hidden
by this allocator optimization.
