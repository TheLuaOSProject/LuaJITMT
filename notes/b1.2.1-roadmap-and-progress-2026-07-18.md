# b1.2.1 roadmap and progress snapshot

Date: 2026-07-18

This is a dated implementation inventory, not a change to `plan/` and not a
claim that the full lockless project is complete. Percentages below are
engineering estimates based on landed behavior and missing acceptance
evidence; they are not measured coverage or calendar commitments.

## Position after b1.2.0

`b1.2.0` deliberately established the narrower functionality-beta gate: GC2
and JIT run together without a known ordinary breaking bug or catastrophic
slowdown. The long-term requirements remain fully nonblocking, thread-safe
ordinary GC, JIT, VM and FFI operation, stock Lua/LuaJIT semantics and API/ABI
compatibility, and performance near or better than stock LuaJIT.

At `43a597b3`, the branch contains 22 commits after the `b1.2.0` tag. The
principal landed b1.2.1 work is:

- the Windows GC2 hot capability lookup moved from MinGW emulated TLS into
  admitted per-thread cells;
- generic x64 `CALLXS` acquired exact trace pins, certified native-frame GC
  scanning, XSAVE/POSTCALL lifecycle, callback suspension, remote GC/flush
  coverage, production scalar and rooted boxed results, and indirect aggregate
  sret results; and
- the dormant helpable table-rescan primitive now uses one exact descriptor
  generation for transfer and completion, eliminating the delayed-helper
  request-recreation race.

The generic `CALLXS` lifecycle for admitted shapes is roughly 90% complete,
but full ABI and caller-topology coverage is closer to 60-70%. The exact
table-token primitive is complete at its own layer; the production table-rescan
cutover remains only about 20-30% complete because the legacy pending state and
counter are still the live authority.

## Critical implementation order

### 1. Live helpable table-rescan cutover

Complete bounded small-arena and HugeTab token enumeration, exact body or
terminal admission, scan-proof publication, requested-generation/full-pass
acknowledgement, and joined-world preflight. Then migrate publication,
traversal completion and every phase-close predicate coherently from the
publisher-owned `table_rescan_pending` count to the descriptor/token plane.

The cutover must cover delayed helpers and scanners, concurrent refresh,
same-address reuse, logical-free/deferred-free terminal completion, token and
descriptor saturation, physical reuse/unmap exclusion, queue failure, and a
publisher racing an empty close pass. The descriptor and lane remain dormant
until these pieces compose; a partial live authority would be less safe than
the current conservative counter.

### 2. Remove peer-dependent waits from ordinary core paths

The post-release FFI work did not yet reduce the broad wait inventory. At this
snapshot the source still contains 42 `lj_gc2_smr_read_enter`, 69
`lj_tab_wait_no_l`, 51 `lj_tab_store_wait_l`, 25 `gc2_peer_wait_*`, 16
`lj_jit_token_acquire_wait`, and 55 `futex_wait` textual occurrences, including
definitions and allowed boundaries.

Every ordinary VM/JIT/GC/FFI caller must be converted to bounded try/restart,
helpable publication, asynchronous request, or conservative retain/defer.
Explicit program-selected join/mutex/channel waits, `require`, `ffi.cdef`,
debugger/profiler control and joined terminal teardown remain the named
blocking boundaries. A source allowlist and paused-owner runtime tests must
prove that no forbidden helper remains reachable from ordinary work.

### 3. Complete generic FFI ABI and caller topology

Remaining x64 work includes recursive SysV eightbyte classification, register
rollback, small and mixed multi-register aggregate results, by-value aggregate
arguments, and Win64 scalar-sized aggregates, caller-owned by-reference
temporaries, positional/shadow/sret and FP-vararg rules. Complex/vector shapes
and non-x64 lowering remain later platform work.

The physical-call lifecycle must also cover protected and continuation roots,
root tailcalls, direct-vararg frames, terminal returns and non-Lua immediate
callers. The known Win64 UCRT/Wine nested-callback unwind failure must be fixed
and gated. This remains generic ABI lowering; the removed explicit C-call
shape matcher must not return in another form.

### 4. Close ordinary FFI, VM and JIT wait surfaces

The certified `CALLXS` path is nonwaiting under its admitted tests, but callback
attachment, CType readers outside the allowed `ffi.cdef` writer boundary,
FFI registry/table publication, ordinary table resize/store, string-table
publication, JIT token/trace-vector access and safepoint leadership still need
their full nonwaiting conversions.

### 5. Complete promised correctness evidence

Add the deterministic traced-CNEW allocation-to-snapshot-restoration pause,
durable deferred full-GC requests across peer finalizers, adversarial racy-Lua
semantic schedules, and repeated worker/JIT/finalizer stress under assertions,
paranoia, ASan and UBSan. Preserve the no-retired-GC-runtime source and runtime
gates permanently.

## Secondary release work

- Platform completion is roughly 55-65%. Windows hot lookup is landed, while
  cold admitted-cell lifecycle/DLL detach and Win64 unwind remain. macOS and
  Windows need platform runtime, ABI and concurrency stress beyond artifact
  smoke, using native CI plus Wine/Darling where appropriate.
- Performance is roughly 35-45% of the parity goal. The temporary default
  comparator still guards only a 100x cliff. The latest release evidence put
  `tab_insert_newkey` at 9.45x and `closures_upval` at 8.39x stock, while many
  other rows were close to parity. Restore the previous 3x hard ceiling first,
  add an MT GC-churn floor, then tighten toward parity.
- Physical minor GC has substantial remembered/barrier substrate, but both
  production gates remain disabled and generational requests use documented
  major fallback. Enabling it requires the remaining parent-qualified edge
  audit and adversarial FUNC/UPVAL/UDATA/PROTO/TRACE mutation evidence.
- Arbitrary custom `lua_Alloc` remains a documented temporary exclusion. The
  ABI entry points remain visible, callbacks are intentionally ignored, and
  `LJ_GC2_INTERNAL_ALLOCATOR_ONLY` plus its tests must stay mandatory until a
  generalized metadata/lifetime design lands.
- The Lua `atomic` library is sequenced after the core collector/JIT/FFI
  cutovers, not cancelled. It is currently unimplemented; the internal C
  atomic layer is not the requested `atomic(4)`, `:load()`, store, exchange and
  compare/exchange Lua API.

## Proposed b1.2.1 release gate

1. The production table descriptor/token plane passes the complete delayed
   helper/scanner, reuse/unmap, terminal-state, saturation and close-race
   matrix under focused concurrency, paranoia, ASan and UBSan.
2. A mechanical allowlist proves ordinary VM/JIT/GC/FFI paths contain no
   peer-dependent wait; paused-owner tests prove bounded caller completion.
3. Every admitted generic-FFI ABI class and caller topology has exact
   no-replay/effect-count, callback, STOPREQ, remote GC and flush coverage on
   SysV and Win64, with no known callback-unwind failure.
4. The full milestone, stock interpreter/JIT, no-JIT, no-FFI, worker,
   finalizer and no-legacy-runtime matrices pass from clean builds.
5. Linux, macOS and Windows platform runtime and ABI gates pass. Cross-runtime
   Wine/Darling evidence supplements rather than replaces available native CI.
6. The catastrophic 100x performance gate is replaced by a reproducible
   three-sample ceiling of at most 3x stock plus an MT progress floor, with
   remaining parity debt reported explicitly.
7. Physical minor collection is either enabled with its proof or remains an
   explicit major-fallback boundary. Custom-allocator generalization and the
   Lua `atomic` module remain visible open work until they land; any staged
   release boundary must be stated explicitly and must not count them as
   complete.

## Estimate

Excluding the later-sequenced Lua `atomic` module and temporarily omitted
custom-allocator generalization, the core b1.2.1 engineering scope is
approximately 40-45% complete. Including those still-required features gives
roughly 30-35%. Release readiness is approximately 30-40% because the live
table-token cutover, zero-wait gate, complete ABI/topology matrix, Win64 unwind,
platform stress and meaningful performance threshold still dominate the
remaining risk.

## 2026-07-19 addendum

The branch has moved substantially beyond the snapshot above. Bounded small
and Huge table-token enumeration, non-ABA destructive Huge admission,
saturating authorities, full-pass certification, current-generation scalar
store resolution, and a common `ANY`/`EXISTING`/`NIL` keyed CAS are now in the
implementation. GC-valued scalar stores use a live guard with retained
parent/key/value leases, weak-write protection, a per-TG root descriptor,
root-gate admission/revalidation, dirty invalidation, and durable rescan
handoff. Weak clearing now retries across real array/hash resize cutovers.

The generic FFI direction has also advanced: the large explicit C-call shape
matcher remains removed, x64 CALLXS covers scalar/bool/rooted boxed and
indirect aggregate results, and CLibrary close now has nonwaiting OPEN/CLOSING
read admission plus terminal handle deferral for escaped cdata/JIT pointers.
Table-valued FFI `__newindex`, library stores, and x64 helper barriers converge
on the common table transaction. `luaL_newmetatable` winner transfer is rooted
and exact-result-leased across both initial lookup and racing NIL-CAS paths.

The adversarial audit also made the next blockers more precise:

- ordinary public point readers, NEXT/iteration, length, and direct VM/JIT
  table reads still have raw vector/result lifetime gaps;
- logical TG owner ids plus main-TG fallback are not an exact physical OS
  actor certificate. b1.2.1 needs a non-reused actor id, atomic `(tid, actor)`
  state claims, TG actor ownership, and explicit moved-close transfer;
- arbitrary C-API-vs-`lua_close` safety additionally needs universe lifetime
  admission rather than actor identity alone;
- new-key collision-chain publication, resize migration, range stores, and
  `table.clear` still need persistent structural/range descriptors;
- forbidden peer-dependent waits remain to be mechanically allowlisted and
  eliminated; and
- recursive SysV/Win64 aggregate ABI, caller topology, Win64 unwind, platform,
  sanitizer, and performance gates remain release work.

With the current scalar-store/weak/CLibrary tranche included, the core scope
excluding the later Lua `atomic` module and temporarily omitted custom
`lua_Alloc` generalization is approximately 45-50% complete. Including those
eventual requirements is approximately 35-40%. Release readiness is roughly
35-45%: the implementation is well past the b1.2.0 “GC2 and JIT basically run”
gate, but the raw-reader, physical-actor, structural-table, zero-wait, remaining
FFI/platform, sanitizer, and release-evidence tranches are still substantive.

## 2026-07-19 physical-actor and rooted-reader checkpoint

The physical-actor blocker above is now substantially implemented on Linux.
TG authority uses a process-issued, non-reused actor identity; `lua_State`
ownership is one atomic `{tid, actor}` capability; GCPREP and release handoffs
retain the exact physical actor; terminal TGs leave the live handoff CAS domain;
and main close migrates the TG/state pair together. Linux creator-exited moved
close uses both a destructor hint and a kernel task-death witness. A live but
quiescent foreign creator still needs a public remotely invalidatable handoff,
and arbitrary API calls racing a freed universe still need universe-lifetime
admission, so this does not close the broader lifetime tranche.

The first production rooted table-reader batch is also implemented. `unpack()`
and `table.concat()` now read elements through exact parent/key/result leases
and enumerated result roots. Existing naked-table C/JIT consumers, default
length discovery, NEXT/iteration, global/environment lookup and VM/JIT fast
reads remain explicit cutover debt. Weak discovery dedupe now publishes one
atomic cycle/state record, so an installing snapshot cannot be mistaken for a
durable weak-list identity.

Strict GCC and Clang builds, the normal and amalgamated no-legacy-runtime gate,
focused actor/handoff/GCPREP/rooted-reader/weak tests, the full GC2 traversal
fixture, and the string canonical-quarantine suite pass for this checkpoint.
The core estimate remains approximately 45-50% excluding the later `atomic`
and custom-allocator work, while release readiness is now approximately 40-45%.
The next critical path is the rest of the rooted table/VM/JIT reads, followed
by structural mutation descriptors and the mechanical zero-wait gate.

## 2026-07-19 rooted meta/VM/JIT/FFI checkpoint

The next point-read tranche is now implemented. Public get/set/raw table APIs
retain authoritative stack and pseudo-index sources across retries and physical
stack growth. Rooted metamethod chains carry receiver, key, method and result
through table- and function-valued chains. The x64 raw-read, `TGETR`, `GGET`
and `GSET` paths use rooted helpers, including current-function environment
replacement. Active-MT recording and generated point reads use enumerated
temporary roots and the rooted table helper rather than naked array/hash loads;
mutable global/metatable cases without a complete runtime guard fail closed.

CType metamethod lookup is bounded and rooted across parser/topology changes,
table generations and GC lifetime. Interpreter consumers transfer table and
finalizer values into natural stack roots before dropping the private CType
root, while unsupported mutable recorder cases fail closed. Function-pointer
topology remains generic and the removed explicit C-call/FFI-shape matcher has
not returned.

Strict GCC and Clang assertion/helper builds pass with `-Wall -Werror`, as do
the focused rooted API/meta/x64/JIT/FFI suites, the full GC2 traversal fixture,
and normal plus amalgamated no-retired-runtime gates. A Windows UCRT x64
cross-build and Wine rooted-global smoke also pass. One important lifetime debt
remains: an asynchronous STOPREQ/OOM/overflow thrown from a dynamic anchor
scope may be caught by an inner Lua fast `pcall()` before the outer C unwind
checkpoint observes it. A protected-frame anchor checkpoint or no-throw helper
cutover is required; deterministic semantic error paths are balanced now.

At this checkpoint the core scope is approximately 50-55% complete excluding
the later Lua `atomic` module and temporarily omitted custom `lua_Alloc`
generalization, or approximately 40-45% including them. Release readiness is
approximately 45-50%. The critical path is now structural length/NEXT/new-key/
resize/range descriptors, the mechanical zero-wait cutover, remaining generic
FFI ABI/caller topologies and callback unwind, then sanitizer/platform/release
evidence and performance tightening.

## 2026-07-25 bounded rooted length/JIT checkpoint

One more rooted table-reader tranche is now implemented. Active-MT generated
table-length reads retain an authoritative `TValue` root and call a single-
attempt helper that validates the exact physical owner, table allocation, raw
root, and paired array/hash generation. A failed admission or changed fact
returns a retry sentinel and side exits before consuming a result. The helper
does not wait, yield, allocate, or throw. Before MT activation the recorder
continues to emit the stock `IR_ALEN`, preserving the normal single-threaded
hot trace.

Focused tests cover generation replacement at the length linearization point,
root replacement, owner transfer, retiring generations, lease failure,
generated side exit and interpreter replay, cleanup accounting, and absence of
table-wait counter movement inside the bounded helper. IR gates prove that the
pre-MT trace remains stock and the supported active-MT trace has an enumerated
temporary root, rooted call, retry guard, and no naked `IR_ALEN`. Strict GCC,
Clang, no-JIT, Lua 5.2 compatibility, focused ASan/UBSan, Windows x64
cross/Wine, and macOS x64 cross/Darling checks pass for the tranche.

This checkpoint deliberately does not close the ordinary length zero-wait
item: a generated retry replays in the interpreter, whose legacy
`lj_tab_len_rooted()` loop can still reach `lj_tab_wait_l()`. A preliminary
single-run active-MT microbenchmark was also about 23x slower than the stock
pre-MT `IR_ALEN` path (roughly 64 ns versus 2.8 ns), although the pre-MT
generated path is unchanged. Both the final wait removal and a cheaper
active-MT validated fast path remain release work.

The overall estimates above remain unchanged at their stated granularity.
Next table work should establish bounded interpreter redispatch/help for
paired structural generations and a persistent helpable new-key publication
descriptor, then use those stable facts for resize/range descriptors and
active-MT fast-path optimization. See
`notes/bounded-rooted-table-length-jit-2026-07-25.md` for the exact transaction,
coverage, and residual boundary.

Development sequencing remains Linux-first. The complete macOS and Windows
test/fix matrix is deferred until b1.2.1 is otherwise release-ready; incidental
cross-build or compatibility smokes before then do not expand the active
platform scope.

## 2026-07-25 active-MT length performance checkpoint

The bounded generated table-length path now has a separate x64 JIT-only ABI.
It requires the exact `TMPREF IN1` root in the universe-aware current TG,
published JIT entry, exact physical actor/state ownership, and a caller-owned
table-vector epoch. JLOOP's live `jit_base` keeps GC bodies retained across the
call; the epoch retains any paired array/hash generation captured by the
helper. Root, generation, carrier and ownership are revalidated before the
integer result escapes. Direct/interpreter calls fail closed, and the general
rooted helper remains available for adversarial roots and non-x64 fallback.

This removes the counted table lease and global SMR reader from the x64
generated hot path without weakening the lockless contract. On a core-pinned
single-core nine-sample run of 20 million traced `#table` operations, active-MT
length fell from 64.125 ns to 9.332 ns. The stock pre-MT `IR_ALEN` path remains
unchanged at 2.461 ns. Thus the new path is about 6.87x faster than the first
bounded implementation and 3.79x the stock lowering; the remaining
absolute/ratio gap stays explicit performance debt.

Focused coverage includes direct misuse rejection, actual generated admission,
generation-change retry and side-exit replay, exact nested epoch restoration,
unchanged wait/allocation/lease accounting, `TMPREF IN1`, and the absence of
active-MT `IR_ALEN`. This is a Linux-first checkpoint; macOS and Windows remain
deferred until the rest of b1.2.1 is release-ready.

The final Linux source passed clean focused M5/M6 gates, 20 repeated C-fixture
runs, five repeated IR runs, 30 heavy six-writer/two-GC-worker resize runs, and
the broader resize/JIT-read/weak-finalizer/remote-stack/worker-activation
matrix. Strict GCC and Clang `-Wall -Werror` builds passed. Clang ASan and
UBSan each passed their strict build, 20 focused repetitions, ten heavy stress
repetitions, and the mixed concurrency matrix before the default build was
restored.

The interpreter wait is not closed by this optimization. Audit proved that a
paused resize owner can replace a source with `FORWARD` before installing the
value in its successor, with the only surviving value held in an owner-local C
variable. Re-yielding or redispatching cannot manufacture that fact. Exact
paused-owner completion needs a persistent resize descriptor published before
retirement/forwarding, with durable per-slot moving state and helper-safe
successor accounting.

The safest structural precursor is a versioned help lane for ordinary shared
new-key insertion. Its descriptor must publish immutable table/key/generation
facts before the lane becomes visible and use intent-before-side-effect phases
for freecount reservation, `KEYLOCK`, collision linking, key publication and
rescan handoff. Pre-MT/private insertion stays stock. FINREG remains a separate
follow-on because its indistinguishable `FINCLAIM` also transfers into the
order-list/finalizer transaction and cannot be closed honestly by the ordinary
new-key descriptor alone.

## 2026-07-25 monotonic shared new-key checkpoint

Research and bounded modeling found a smaller ordinary-new-key protocol than
the versioned help lane proposed above. On the Linux/x86-64 active-MT path, an
ordinary inserter now publishes the canonical real key directly with a CAS and
treats that successful CAS as an irreversible, owner-only claim. Collision
claims are linked with an exact-current-tail NULL-to-node CAS. Every shared
chain publisher follows that same exact-tail discipline, so publishers for one
chain contend on its current tail; the FINREG test helper was changed from
head-prepend to tail-append accordingly. Private/pre-MT insertion is unchanged.

This removes ordinary `KEYLOCK` publication and its peer-dependent owner
window without adding a descriptor. Same-key collision losers and stale
unlinked collision generations leave nil-valued canonical claimants for resize
to compact; a reachable empty-anchor claimant may instead be filled by a peer.
A tail-CAS loser retains and retries the same claimed node. The uncontended
collision path still uses three locked RMWs: freecount, real-key claim, and tail
link. Seven interleaved core-pinned measurements against exact pre-change
commit `c832d41d` put the new median about 0.36% faster, within noise. This is
no-regression evidence against the fork baseline; a stock LuaJIT binary was
not available for a stock-parity measurement.

Deterministic coverage now exercises empty-anchor and collision same-key
races, different-key tail-CAS loss, ordinary/FINREG tail contention, a live
suffix appended through a paused claimant's physical-anchor role, resize of
that suffix while the claimant is paused, transition from a historical private
chain, and quiescent whole-vector graph validation. The focused fixture passed
100 repeated runs. Strict GCC/Clang, ASan, UBSan, the focused FINREG/KEYLOCK/
chain/resize cases, and the heavier table-resize stress also pass. Bounded
exhaustive and randomized models found no cycle, duplicate live key, lost
completed store, or progress failure in the modeled schedules.

This checkpoint does not complete structural locklessness. FINREG still has a
`KEYLOCK`/`FINCLAIM` owner-stall window and needs a persistent helpable
descriptor. Resize can still replace a source value with `FORWARD` while the
only payload is in its owner's C local, so it needs a durable descriptor before
retirement begins. Canonical nil-valued claimant capacity, conservative rehash
sizing, physical-anchor suffix chain amplification, and portable non-x64
cross-address ordering remain explicit performance/portability debts. The
insertion benchmark does not replace a stock comparison or a collision-heavy
lookup-locality benchmark, and deterministic collectable-key coverage of the
CAS-to-barrier window remains test debt. Full macOS and Windows validation and
fixes remain deferred until b1.2.1 is otherwise release-ready.

See `notes/monotonic-shared-newkey-2026-07-25.md` for the protocol, proof
obligations, coverage, measurements, and residual boundaries. The next
structural tranche is the persistent resize descriptor, followed by FINREG's
separate helpable publication transaction.
