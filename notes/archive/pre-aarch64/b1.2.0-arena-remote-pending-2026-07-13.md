# b1.2.0 arena remote-free pending hint

## Scope and profile

This checkpoint removes the remaining superlinear empty-queue component from
GC-stopped small allocation.  When a private bump window was exhausted,
`lj_arena_remote_free_drain()` walked every arena in both owner lists even when
no remote free had ever been published.  The number of empty arena probes
therefore grew with both the number of mapped arenas and the number of bump
refills.

The change was developed against `ce46f22b` and rebased after the bump-priority
allocator fix.  It does not alter an allocation, free-run, lifetime, root,
recovery, or sweep transition.

## Protocol

`TGAlloc.remote_pending` is a 32-bit advisory wake for the arena-local
intrusive remote-free queues owned by that allocator.  All production access
uses the dedicated acquire/release/exchange helper surface.

A producer which actually inserts a queue record performs, in order:

1. the existing release CAS into `GCAhdr.remote_free`;
2. release of its arena admission/plain mutation generation; and
3. a release store of `remote_pending = 1`.

Publishing the wake after the arena admission is essential.  If the producer
published it before `arena_remote_leave()`, a consumer could clear the wake,
observe the nonzero publisher count, reject the arena, and permanently strand
the queue record.

An opportunistic consumer performs one acquire-release exchange of the hint to
zero.  Zero returns immediately without reading an arena list.  A nonzero
result scans the existing `owned[]` lists exactly as before.  Clearing before
the scan gives the required no-drop cases:

- a producer whose queue and wake precede the exchange is covered by the
  exchange's acquire edge and this scan;
- a producer after the exchange leaves the hint set for the next scan, even if
  this scan happens to see and consume its record;
- a producer paused between queue CAS and wake store may be consumed by an
  overlapping forced/older scan, but its later store conservatively schedules
  another scan; and
- several producers may coalesce their stores to one without losing work,
  because all queue CAS operations precede their respective stores.

If a consumer finds a durable queue but an unrelated arena admission or sweep
transition prevents draining it, the consumer release-rearms the allocator
wake before returning.  This is required because a rescue/late actor which did
not enqueue a remote free has no reason to publish another queue wake when it
leaves.

The hint is deliberately not a queue count and correctness never depends on it
being an exact reflection of queue emptiness.

## Force paths and lifecycle audit

There is one intrusive queue publication site, in
`lj_arena_remote_free_publish()`.  Every other successful early return is a
bit-only late/sweep/quarantine ownership path and creates no queue record.

Three consumers have different authority:

- ordinary allocation uses the advisory `lj_arena_remote_free_drain()` and
  may take the O(1) empty return;
- sweep already owns an exact arena and continues to call
  `lj_arena_remote_free_drain_sweep()` directly, independent of the hint; and
- joined-world owner transfer and terminal prefree use
  `lj_arena_remote_free_drain_force()`, which clears any stale wake but always
  scans the owner lists.

Transfer retains its existing dead/quiescent-source precondition.  It force
drains before changing arena ownership, rebuilds the destination bins, and
atomically carries any conservative post-drain rearm into the destination
instead of stranding it in the dead source allocator.  Queues on already
detached sweep/quarantine arenas remain covered by their exact-arena sweep
consumer.  Terminal prefree force drains every internal TG allocator after the
existing worker/publisher join checks and before terminal gate reconciliation.
`lj_arena_alloc_init()` zeroes the hint with the rest of the allocator; a
retained failed `fini` keeps its conservative hint and lists, while a complete
`fini` reinitializes it.

## Deterministic proof and regression coverage

`tests/t-arena-remote-pending.c`, wired into `m2_arena_publication`, adds test
pauses and counters which are compiled only with `LJ_ARENA_TEST_HELPERS`.  It
proves:

- an empty opportunistic drain across more than four arenas performs one fast
  skip and zero arena probes;
- queue publication paused before wake publication is not lost;
- a publisher after the consumer's clear leaves a conservative next wake;
- a queue blocked by an unrelated rescue admission rearms its consumed wake;
- eight simultaneous publishers coalesce without dropping any free;
- transfer force draining ignores a cleared hint and the destination reuses
  the exact cell;
- sweep drains an exact arena with a cleared hint; and
- the terminal force primitive drains an owned queue with a cleared hint.

The focused race fixture passes under Clang 19 ASan+UBSan with leak detection.
The complete `m2_arena_all` suite passes, including publication, realloc,
allocf, hugetab, sweep, runtime GC sweep, and assertion close coverage.

## Linux/x86-64 performance

Pinned to CPU 7, JIT enabled, GC stopped, `_exit()` excluding shutdown, the
benchmark repeatedly stored a fresh three-element table into a retained table
slot so every allocation escaped trace sinking.  Three-run median CPU times
were:

| allocations | exact base | pending hint | improvement |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1,000,000 | 645.1 ns/op | 612.6 ns/op | 5.0% |
| 2,000,000 | 688.8 ns/op | 614.3 ns/op | 10.8% |
| 3,000,000 | 736.9 ns/op | 616.2 ns/op | 16.4% |

The optimized result remains approximately flat from one to three million
allocations, while the exact base cost rises with the accumulated empty arena
walks.  Real remote-free work retains the original per-arena protocol and cost.
