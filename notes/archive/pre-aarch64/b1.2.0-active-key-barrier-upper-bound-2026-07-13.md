# b1.2.0 active-key barrier upper bound (2026-07-13)

This was a no-change investigation on `72ac7754`.  It records why further
table-key barrier micro-optimization is not currently a useful b1.2.0 path.

## Baseline

Same-session release builds measured unique string-key insertion at roughly:

- 5,000 keys: 324.6 ns/op;
- 20,000 keys: 522.05 ns/op;
- 40,000 keys: 713.6 ns/op.

Stock LuaJIT was about 61--85 ns/op at 5,000 keys, 79.5 ns/op at 20,000,
and 81.7--83.6 ns/op at 40,000.  The fork also continues to scale badly at
larger counts while GC2 phases remain active.

## Unsafe upper-bound experiment

An intentionally unsafe diagnostic build made both `lj_gc_pubtabkey_()` and
`strtab_mark_before_publish()` unconditional no-ops.  It measured 321.8,
492.45, and 696.35 ns/op for the same three sizes; 100,000 keys improved only
from roughly 2.1 us/op to 1.96 us/op.  Thus deleting all key/string publication
work cannot approach the b1.2.0 `3.0x` stock ceiling.

The following exact prototypes were also measured and rejected because their
effect was noise or negative:

- removing the duplicated outer key validator;
- a full `lj_gc2_ismarked()` SWEEP precheck;
- a per-TG active-black/current-cycle returned-string certificate;
- a migration-only current-mark/non-retired certificate; and
- a protected string-table current-mark shortcut.

The per-TG certificate covered about 54.6% of 20,000-key barriers, and the
migration extension covered about 93.6% at 40,000 keys, without material
throughput improvement.  No production or test change from these prototypes
was retained.

## Conclusion

Barrier work is only a small fraction of this row.  The release-blocking cost
is dominated by GC phase/JIT admission, trace exit/re-entry, and allocation
assist cadence.  Re-evaluate key publication only after the cooperative JIT
composition changes that scheduling bound.
