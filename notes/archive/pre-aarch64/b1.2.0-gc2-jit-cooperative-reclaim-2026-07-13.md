# b1.2.0 GC2/JIT cooperative reclaim checkpoint

This checkpoint changes the b1.2.0 JIT/GC2 boundary from “all native execution
is excluded for the complete collection” to cooperative native turns during the
physically safe part of SWEEP. MARK admission, MARK, WEAK, root snapshots,
phase transitions, and every physical-reclaim quantum still exclude compiled
entry. No collector waits for an active trace or an ordinary blocking FFI call.

## Entry and close protocol

On x86-64, all three trace-entry paths use `gc2.jit_phase_gate` as the sole hot
admission word. Gate-open invariants are:

- `gate == 1` implies GC2 is IDLE, or SWEEP has published its complete READY
  root boundary;
- every transition which invalidates that predicate closes the gate first;
- entry observes the gate, publishes TG-local `jit_base` with a memory `xchg`
  (an SC locked RMW), then rechecks the gate before reading TraceVec, a trace
  slot, a trace body, or mcode;
- the close owner publishes zero and executes the matching SC fence before its
  zero-active sample.

Thus an entry either rejects the close, or the close owner observes its intent
and defers. Gate denial publishes the durable `jit_sweep_displaced` demand
latch. A SWEEP reopen consumes that latch with exchange and grants a bounded
50-us mutator lease. Lease expiry is observational: a pre-claim observer never
stores zero over a newer worker-owned lease; the next owned close clears it.

All optimized x86-64 loops contain an `IR_XPOLL`. The cheap form checks only the
gate; the extended form also checks the TG poll/profile word. Pure arithmetic
loops therefore exit asynchronously when a worker closes the gate, while an
ordinary FFI call may finish in its own time and publish quiescence on normal
trace exit.

The common allocation-accounting checkpoint can consume the hard cadence
before generated code reaches `lj_gc_step_jit()`. It now issues the same
asynchronous SWEEP gate close after bounded assist has released worker
ownership and before advancing `hard_check_bytes`. This retains a no-worker,
allocation-heavy trace progress path.

## IDLE and SWEEP reclaim ownership

IDLE retire reclaim now wins `smr_reclaiming`, owns an exact gate `1 -> 0` CAS,
executes the SC close fence, and samples every JIT TG inactive before releasing
any retired string/table/ctype/clib/trace/mcode state. The gate stays closed for
the complete destruction scope. A failed or completed owner attempts `0 -> 1`
only while it still holds `smr_reclaiming`, GC2 is exactly IDLE, all reclaim
readiness predicates hold, and no cycle request owns pre-MARK admission. A
pending request inherits the closed gate and wakes a later MARK worker.

The three phase-owner transitions to IDLE keep their already-SC-closed gate
through the IDLE handshake. A same-thread nested retire pass records that gate
as borrowed: it may acquire the exclusive SMR writer and destroy retired state,
but it never reopens a gate owned by the outer transition. This avoids both a
self-deadlock and an early reopen between phase publication and the transition
owner's final IDLE checks.

`gc2_mark_begin()` rechecks `smr_reclaiming == 0` after closing entry and
sampling inactive JIT TGs but before typed activation staging or any MARK
mutation. This completes the inverse race: whichever actor wins retains its
own closure and the losing actor performs no partial cycle-start effects.

SWEEP physical reclaim requires READY, a closed gate, zero active JIT TGs, the
worker token, the exclusive SMR writer, and the typed reclaim capability both
before and after publication. The physical-reclaim scope retains closure
through every destructor; only the scheduler may reopen after the complete
bounded quantum has left reclaim ownership. The worker/open-close scheduler
alternates bounded mutator leases with bounded reclaim quanta and does not
synchronously request `EXIT_TRACES`.

## Nonwaiting original-bytecode recovery

Gate denial is normal, so it cannot map a patched opcode to `ins_next`. In
particular, JLOOP can replace LOOP, ITERN, RET, RET0, or RET1, while JFORL and
JITERL have already applied their loop-state update before common entry.

Every prototype therefore contains a fixed recovery sidecar with one `BCIns`
per bytecode instruction. Constructors initialize it to zero. Trace publication
release-stores the immutable original instruction into the exact sidecar slot
before release-publishing the patched bytecode. Zero is safe as the unpublished
sentinel because the root-patch input set is FORL/LOOP, ITERL/FUNCF, ITERN and
RET*, never the all-zero ISLT instruction. Retracing asserts that a nonzero slot
never changes.

Gate-denied fallback resolves the sidecar from the live Lua frame/prototype and
executes the exact original instruction without reading TraceVec/trace bodies,
taking global SMR, or waiting for the retire owner. This also covers the ISNEXT
despecialization path: a failed specialized `next` check recovers ITERN from the
sidecar and publishes ITERC without its former ungated trace-slot read.
JFUNCF no longer performs a pre-common TraceVec probe; it always routes through
the gate/`xchg`/identity-validation path.

The sidecar is a deliberate b1.2.0 correctness/performance tradeoff, not the
final layout. Controlled measurements found about +19.6% retained-prototype
memory for tiny functions, +44.8% for medium functions, +50.2% for large
functions, and about 18.3% on a synthetic build/load/introspection workload.
b1.2.1 must replace this dense array with a lazy/sparse immutable recovery map
without weakening the nonwaiting proof. Parser and bytecode-loader allocation,
both prototype geometry validators, exact colocated pointer validation, free by
`sizept`, and dump/load roundtrips have been updated/audited for the b1.2.0
layout.

## Focused verification

- `t-jit-idle-reclaim-entry`: a foreign IDLE reclaimer pauses after its
  post-SC zero-active sample and before retired-slot release. While the writer
  remains paused, real Lua execution completes exact JFUNCF, LOOP, RET1, ITERN,
  JFORL, and JITERL results with no native hits. It also forces one temporary
  recovery retry to prove the local fallback does not repeat an induction or
  iterator update, and replaces global `next` to exercise closed-gate ISNEXT
  despecialization. Native entry resumes after owned reopen.
- `t-gc2-jit-sweep-coop`: a real allocating trace executes during SWEEP+READY,
  a foreign close returns without waiting for a pure active loop, XPOLL exits
  it, and bounded reclaim reaches IDLE. Its helper build explicitly sets zero
  workers, observes an open gate, seeds a one-byte-short TG accounting batch
  and a due hard cadence, then attributes the close to the common allocation
  checkpoint and verifies `hard_check_bytes` advanced. Ten consecutive focused
  helper runs passed.
- `t-jit-token`: updated XPOLL shape/token fixture passed.
- A 20-million-call tiny JFUNCF-style interpreter-to-compiled-function probe
  measured a 0.1766-s fork median versus 0.1153 s for system LuaJIT (about
  1.53x). A separate cycle-controlled comparison measured the sole-gate memory
  `xchg` entry at about 1.13x the no-gate baseline; the previous
  phase/READY+`mfence` path was about 2.99x.

After reconstruction onto the later recovery/frame-PC checkpoint, the focused
matrix passed 20 paused-IDLE runs, 10 zero-worker/helper SWEEP runs, default
SWEEP, activation-veto, worker-scheduler, XPOLL/token, GC2 readiness, ordinary
FFI blocking-call, active-stack GC, and both normal and assert/paranoia no-drop
recovery fixtures. Active-thread roots, GC statistics, weak modes with JIT on
and off, assertion-build close churn, and the 509-test stock suite also passed.
Generated `lj_vm.o` contains exactly three plain-memory `xchg` publications to
TG `jit_base` and no `mfence`.

The aggregate `m6_jit_alloc_account` currently stops before the new fixtures at
the pre-existing normal-hard TNEW fixtop expectation. A detached exact-parent
`7e4c2c86` helper build fails that same assertion, while both new fixtures pass
directly on the reconstructed tree. This remains separate pacing-test debt and
is not classified as a cooperation regression.

This checkpoint does not reopen MARK or WEAK. In particular, traced raw weak
table stores do not yet publish the complete pre-store `weak_write_active`
window required by concurrent weak clearing. MARK-only cooperative admission is
a separate follow-up after its root/barrier proof; WEAK remains closed.
